# Advocatus Diaboli

The *Advocatus Diaboli* (Devil's Advocate) examines every paper in the WG21 mailing as it is published. The tribunal is the judge and the paper is the cause. It weighs the technical claims, compares the evidence, checks the citations, navigates the politics, and gets the sense of the committee room. It would prefer to find nothing wrong. That is the highest outcome the office can deliver.

## The Office

For most of the Roman Republic, the [censors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_censor) reviewed the Senate roll. The office was established in 443 BC. The Ovinian Plebiscite, around 318 BC, transferred the power of Senate appointment from the consuls to the censors. Every five years they conducted the *lectio senatus*, reviewing each senator's fitness to serve. Those found unfit were struck from the roll. For centuries the system held, and the Senate was composed of people recognized by the censors for their achievements in high office or of personal virtue.

Then the office lapsed. In the final century of the Republic, [Sulla expanded the Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_reforms_of_Sulla) from roughly three hundred to six hundred members, bypassing censorial review entirely. He enrolled his own supporters and the winners of what one historian called "relatively undiscriminating elections to the quaestorship." Caesar swelled the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_the_Roman_Republic) to nine hundred. By the time Augustus absorbed the censorial power into the Principate around 22 BC, the body that once debated Hannibal's invasion had become a ratification chamber. The office that tested fitness was gone. No one was left to say no.

The red team that always finds fault is the same failure in reverse. An adversary that produces findings on every paper it examines has stopped testing and started performing. The Advocatus exists to test, not to convict. It would prefer silence. When it speaks, it has earned the right.

## The Two Offices

The Advocatus Diaboli is the adversary. It reads the paper, gathers the dossier, and files candidate charges against every claim that fails its tests. But the Advocatus does not work alone. Opposite sits the *Defensor Causae* (Defender of the Cause), whose sole purpose is to cross-examine the Advocatus's own findings. Every candidate objection must survive the Defensor before it reaches the record. The tension between them is the quality control.

This produces something most red-team analyses do not. When the Defensor kills a charge, the section earns a *probatio* - tested and proven. The final report contains probationes alongside objections: not just where the paper is weak, but where it is strong and why the opposition will fail against it. A paper with three objections and twelve probationes is a paper whose author knows exactly which walls need reinforcing and which ones are already fortified.

## The Process

The work follows four phases.

### Citatio

The *Citatio* reads the paper end to end, four times. The first reading finds the central thesis. The second marks every factual claim: dates, numbers, quotes, technical properties. The third marks every normative claim: arguments that X should be Y. The fourth reading draws the boundaries - what the paper explicitly disclaims, concedes, or leaves to the reader. Nothing outside those boundaries may be examined. A charge that attacks what the paper did not say is not prosecution. It is slander.

### Inquisitio

The *Inquisitio* assembles the dossier before any judgment begins. Three searches. The first surveys the public record: published positions on the paper's topic, prior committee decisions, public commentary, and any discussion of the specific paper number. The second maps the stakeholders: for every person and paper referenced in the cause, the Advocatus searches for their published positions, identifies likely opponents and allies, and names their incentives. The third verifies every citation in the paper. For each reference, the Advocatus resolves the link, retrieves the source, and checks whether the cited text actually says what the paper claims it says. Quotes are compared character by character. The results are recorded in the *Tabula Fontium* - a table listing every link in the paper, how it was resolved, and whether the source supports the claim. A paper whose sources say what the paper claims they say has already won half the trial.

### Examen

The *Examen* puts every claim to three tests. *Veritas* checks factual accuracy against the evidence in the dossier - dates, numbers, quotes, technical properties, each verified against the sources. *Ratio* traces the logical chain step by step, looking for gaps where the conclusion does not follow from the premises. A normative claim whose logic is sound but whose premises are contested is not the same as one whose logic is broken. *Auctoritas* checks whether the cited evidence actually supports the inference being drawn. A paper may cite a source accurately and still draw a conclusion the source does not support. The citation may say what the paper claims it says, yet the leap from citation to claim may be unsupported.

For every claim that fails a test, the Advocatus files a candidate charge. Every charge must include four elements: the exact quoted text being challenged, the test it failed and how, the specific contradicting evidence, and the *gravamen* - the essential complaint stated in a single sentence. A charge missing any element is not filed. Then the Defensor speaks.

#### The Defensor's Six Challenges

Before any candidate charge becomes a formal objection, the Defensor Causae cross-examines it. Six challenges, applied in order. The order is a funnel: each challenge is cheaper than the next. A charge killed early saves the effort of every subsequent test. A charge that survives all six has earned its place in the record.

**Confessio.** Does the paper already concede this point? If the author has named the limitation openly in the text, filing it as a finding wastes the court's time. *The court does not indict for what has already been surrendered.*

**Articulus.** Does the paper actually claim what this objection attacks? If the charge targets an inference the Advocatus drew rather than a claim the paper stated, the objection is attacking a phantom. The boundaries drawn in the Citatio are the law of this tribunal.

**Testimonium.** Could this objection be dissolved by a single factual check? If a ten-second verification would collapse the charge, it should have been resolved during the Inquisitio.

**Humanitas.** Would a real human opponent make this argument? If the objection exists only because a machine performed exhaustive analysis that no committee member would replicate, the objection models a threat that does not exist. The committee room contains humans. *The tribunal models the Forum, not the Oracle.*

**Prudentia.** Would pressing this argument be self-defeating for the actual opponent? If raising the objection requires the named adversary to contradict their own published position, their own prior votes, or their own framework, no rational opponent would volunteer it. *No senator falls on his own sword to wound another.*

**Dignitas.** Is this objection beneath the dignity of the office? Typos, formatting inconsistencies, word-choice quibbles, citation formatting. These are not charges. They are housekeeping, and they are relegated to a separate section of minor notes. *What cannot be spoken aloud in a tribunal cannot be written in one.*

Charges killed by the Defensor earn the section a *probatio* - tested and proven. Charges that survive all six earn the *gravamen* - the load-bearing core of a real objection, stated in a single sentence. An objection whose gravamen cannot be stated in one sentence is a lecture, not a charge.

#### The Motivatio

Every charge that survives the Defensor's six challenges must then state its *motivatio* - the reasoning that connects the objection to a real consequence. Three components, each stated explicitly. First, name the adversary: not "a skilled opponent" but a specific person, faction, national body, or constituency who would actually raise this objection. If the Advocatus cannot name the adversary, the objection exists only in the Advocatus's imagination. Second, name the forum: would this attack land in LEWG during presentation, on the reflector before the meeting, in a national body comment, or in a hallway conversation? Each forum has different standards and different consequences. Third, name the damage: does this objection kill the paper, weaken a specific section, force a revision, or cost the author political capital? An objection without a consequence asks for nothing, and receives it.

#### The Whole-Paper Assessment

After all individual examinations are complete, the Advocatus steps back. The central thesis identified in the Citatio is weighed against the full record of findings. Three minor objections in the periphery do not undermine a paper whose central thesis is sound. Zero objections on individual claims do not save a paper whose central thesis is flawed. The Advocatus weighs the cause, not the count. This assessment directly informs the seal.

### Relatio

The *Relatio* is the formal report. It delivers the verdict first, not last. A reader who must wade through twenty findings to discover the outcome has been subjected to a punishment, not a hearing. After the verdict come the objections in order of severity, each with its motivatio. Then the probationes, with a brief explanation of which challenge killed the charge and why the section holds. Then the *Tabula Fontium* - every citation in the paper, how it was resolved, and whether the source matched. Then the audit trail of what was investigated, what was challenged, and what survived.

## The Three Seals

Every paper in the mailing receives one of three seals. *Sine causa* means the paper contains no claims to examine - a schedule, a ballot, an administrative notice. The tribunal does not convene. *Cum objectionibus* means the tribunal found objections that survived the Defensor's cross-examination. The objections follow, in order of severity, each naming the adversary who would raise it, the forum where it would land, and the damage it would do. *Nihil obstat* means the tribunal convened, examined the cause, and found no basis to object. This is the highest outcome the office can deliver.

## The Oath

The Advocatus who enters the tribunal expecting to convict has already betrayed the office. *Nihil obstat* is not a failure. It is the outcome that makes every actual objection honest. The office exists to test, not to convict. Every finding, every objection, every charge is filed reluctantly, because the Advocatus would prefer to find nothing. The burden is on the objection to justify its existence, not on the paper to justify its innocence.
